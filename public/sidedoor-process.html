1

Business model, strategy, North Star

Referrers are the scarce side, so nothing is ever sold to them

A two-sided marketplace, built with the Product Anatomy frameworks: business model, then strategy, then design. Written after the research, from the problem statement, not before it.

Phase 1 · Build both sides

Free for everyone

A paywall on an empty marketplace blocks the people it needs.

Phase 2 · Companies pay

For complete, matched referrals

Companies already pay for referrals through their bonus schemes (n62, n87). Guessing that they'd pay a platform.

Candidates, if ever

Only for tools that help them

Never to reach, see or jump ahead of a referrer.

Rejected ways to make money

Rejected

Candidates pay to reach referrers

Version 2's Premium did this. It sells the scarce side's attention, and paying doesn't change a referrer's rule (n50, n76).

Rejected

Referrers pay

They have the least reason to take part already (C4). Charging them kills supply.

Rejected

Ads

Ads push for time in the app. SideDoor's value is a referral done in minutes.

The strategy: quality as identity

Every request that reaches a referrer is complete and a real fit

A referrer can open any request and submit it in minutes, without chasing a single detail. That promise is the problem statement turned around: referrers drop requests that hand them the candidate's work (n76), and help when the candidate has done it (n77). Worked it out

What it costs: candidates do more before they can ask, and can ask fewer people. Some will find that slower than a LinkedIn message. Accepted, because a message nobody reads is slower still.

North Star

The value exchange

A candidate who fits gets referred by someone they didn't know, and that person didn't have to chase anything

A referral counts when either side confirms it went into the company's portal: the referrer marks it, or the candidate marks the company's email. Per referrer, not total, because the referrer is the scarce side and a total can rise from sign-ups alone.

The North Star checklistAnswer
Can it rise while users are unhappy?Partly: poor fits could be submitted. Watched by the guardrail
Can marketing inflate it?No. It's per active referrer
Value, not activity?Yes. A submission is when both sides get what they came for
Would a fall predict trouble?Yes. Fewer referrals per referrer means referrers drifting away

Looks like a North Star, isn't one

Requests sent

Rises when candidates spam, which is the problem SideDoor exists to fix.

Swipes and matches

Version 2's numbers. A match that never becomes a submission delivered nothing.

Messages sent

Rises when details are still being chased, which means the product failed.

The bet

One moment: a stranger asks for a referral, until they hear what happened

The first scope listed five problems at once. The research picked one, and it wasn't the one the first design was built on.

The moment
A candidate asking an employee they don't know for a referral to one job, until the candidate hears what happened to it
Who
Someone a few years into their career who found a job at a company where they know nobody. And the employee who gets the message and has to put them into the company's referral portal
The guess
Employees drop strangers' requests because the request makes them do the candidate's work, not because they won't help a stranger. Worked it out for the cause, Guessing for "referred more often"
The number
% of requests to strangers that the employee submits to their company's portal
Guardrail
Employees who stop accepting referral requests must not rise

My mentor said: make it a LinkedIn feature. I kept it separate.

Why its own product
  • The work sits between two systems nobody owns. It starts on LinkedIn and ends in the company's portal. LinkedIn can't see inside that portal either (n46, n57, n59, n60, n71)
  • The referrer gets value before anyone else joins, from a link they send to strangers (n66)
  • Both people asked for a dedicated place as candidates (n24, n45). Wishes are the weakest evidence

"a link is sent to the candidate to fill out their own personal details, which would cut the referrer's effort by 50%"

Samarth, as a referrer · n66
The risk I kept
  • Both people ask and get asked on LinkedIn today (n04, n09, n29, n49, n68)
  • Referral communities die quiet: on Fishbowl a message might be seen "next week" (n28)
  • The cold start could fail: a referrer may not send a link, because sending it is already a yes

This answer is reasoning. It hasn't been tested.

Not in this project

  • The company's referral bonus: no product controls it (n62, n87)
  • Connecting to referral portals like Workday: no outside product has access (n46)
  • Interviews and HR decisions after the referral (n86, n88)
  • Candidates who take a referral and don't join (n89, n90)
  • Recruiters: no data from them

8

Chosen from three drafts

The request asks the referrer to do the candidate's work, and the answer never travels back

Three drafts: one from the referrer's side, one from the candidate's, one holding both. I picked the one that holds both, and narrowed it.

Problem statement

An employee asked for a referral by someone they don't know has to do the candidate's work before they can say yes. So most don't, and the candidate never finds out.

The request arrives as a LinkedIn message and a resume. The company's portal needs the job ID, the city, the date of birth, career gaps and preferred interview locations, none of which are in the resume. So the referrer either chases the candidate for them or drops the request.

Meanwhile the status of a submitted referral already exists, in the referrer's portal and emails. It never reaches the candidate, who reads silence as a no.

"they don't share the job ID, which irritates me a lot... I will not take the pain and go and check for it"

Riya, as a referrer · n76

"whoever did not reply, they did not refer me"

Riya, as a candidate · n35

Draft A · referrer side

Referring a stranger costs the referrer admin work nobody pays them for, so they don't do it.

Lost: it ignores the candidate who never learns what happened, and that's in both candidate interviews.

What nobody does, and why not

Collect the portal's fields before the ask

Every portal asks something different. The two portals in the notes overlap a lot but not fully, so a request can carry the common fields plus a short company-specific part. Worked it out

Show the candidate what happened

No outside product can read the company's system (n46). But the referrer already sees it (n58, n83), so they pass it on, if that costs a single tap.

Show fit to the referrer

Fit scores are sold to candidates. Samarth checks fit by hand (n50) and asked for exactly this (n65). The real opening.

Keep asking free and still limit volume

GetMeReferred controls volume with price. SideDoor uses a weekly limit and complete-request rules instead.

Do not inherit
  • A free-text request box: more writing, still no job ID
  • Paying per referrer: sells the scarce side
  • Swipe, or Interested / Not interested: one gesture pushes volume
  • Stats on the page with no source
Worth taking
  • "People you can reach out to": the warm path first, as Samarth does (n03, n04)
  • "Did you hear back?": status without the company's data
  • "Review time is typically 1 week": sets the wait before it turns into silence

How much it does on its own, for the fit order

Rejected

Show only matched candidates

A hidden request is silence for the candidate, which is C3 again.

Chosen

The referrer decides every one. No single score: a count they can check, like "4 of 7 skills", each skill with where it was found.

When it's wrong, the words on screen

Not sure

"Not enough in their resume to judge fit for this job. Read it before you decide."

A rule

"Add the job ID first. It's the first thing a referrer needs."

A fault

"We couldn't read this file. Try a PDF with text you can select, or fill in the details yourself."

The riskiest assumption

That a resume and a job description hold enough to judge fit the way Samarth does. If most requests land in "Not enough to judge", the ordering helps no one. The cheapest test: five real resumes for one of his roles, with and without the breakdown.

11

The one-page brief

How might we let a stranger's request be acted on in minutes, and the answer travel back for free?

Domain and user
Job referrals in Indian tech hiring. A candidate asking an employee they don't know for a referral to one job, and the employee being asked
Job to be done
When someone I don't know asks me for a referral, I want everything my company's portal needs already there, so referring takes minutes and they find out what happened without me doing more
Problem statement
How might we let a candidate send a referral request a stranger can act on in minutes, and let the answer reach the candidate without asking the referrer for more work?
Success metric
% of requests to strangers the referrer submits. Guardrail: referrers turning off requests must not rise. North Star: referrals submitted per active referrer per month

The main path

  1. A candidate finds a job where someone refers, with their match shown before asking
  2. Checks the request. The portal's missing fields are asked once, and Send stays off until it's complete
  3. The referrer sees requests ordered by fit, weaker ones folded but still there
  4. Opens one: what could stop it first, then each skill with where it was found
  5. Taps Refer. Every portal field is ready to copy, or emailed to their work inbox
  6. Marks it submitted while the portal is still open
  7. The candidate sees Submitted, and what usually comes next

Written before the test, never changed after

What would prove me wrong

Nobody has used SideDoor yet. The next real test: 8 referrers in tech who got a stranger's request in the last month, each using their link for two weeks.

Dead if

Fewer than 1 in 5

complete requests from strangers get submitted to the company portal. Today's cold message gets about 1 reply in 5: "80% of the time they don't reply" (Riya, n30). A complete request has to beat that.

Dead if

Fewer than 3 of 8

referrers send their link to anyone in the two weeks. Then referrers won't start the loop alone, and the cold-start bet is dead.

First, a usability test

Samarth and Riya, one session each, ten tasks: five as a candidate, then five as a referrer. It can show whether two people can finish the main flows and where they get stuck. It can't show whether referrers will submit more. That needs the test above.

Open questions a real test would answer

How much of a real portal form do the common fields cover?

Will a referrer pass on the status if it's one tap?

Does showing fit to the referrer raise how many they submit, or only how fast they decline?

Are invited candidates referred as often as those who ask on their own?
