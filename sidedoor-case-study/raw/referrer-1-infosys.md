1. Have you referred someone before? Yes, I have referred quite a few candidates for my company.

2. Where exactly are you working right now? Right now, I am working at Infosys as a Specialist Programmer in an L2 role.

3. Could you please quantify the number of people you have referred? I have referred approximately 10 people.

4. Out of those 10, approximately how many were known to you and how many were strangers? Approximately 8 were known to me, and 2 were strangers.

5. How do people usually reach out to you for referrals? Known contacts usually reach out through personal chat platforms like WhatsApp. Strangers typically request referrals via LinkedIn.

6. How do you decide whether to refer someone or not for both known people and strangers? The criteria are similar for both. First, there must be at least a 50% to 70% skill match. Second, their work experience must match the requirement, which is non-negotiable. I also look deeply into their resume to ensure their listed work experience actually demonstrates the required skill set.

7. Have you ever referred someone who did not match properly or whose experience was lagging? No, in my company, you have to provide specific data like the current experience of the person. If the experience does not match, there is no point in referring them because the profile will just get rejected.

8. Do you feel you are a little bit more lenient to people who are known to you while referring? Yes, I am a little bit more lenient with known people and sometimes go by the luck factor, hoping it works out well for them.

9. Have you ever referred someone just for the bonus or cash benefits? Not entirely, but if a candidate is a borderline match, I might consider referring them to see if it works out.

10. What do you look for before saying yes to a referral? I ensure their years of experience and skill sets match. Their work experience should accurately reflect the skills required for the role rather than being from a completely different domain.

11. Have you ever avoided referring someone, and if yes, why? I have not avoided or ghosted anyone. I try to be very open with candidates; if their profile does not match the requirements, I openly give them feedback so they know we are not moving forward.

12. Do you worry about your own reputation while referring in your company? No, I do not worry about that. I believe that if someone turns out to be a bad hire after going through the interview rounds, that responsibility should not fall on the referrer.

13. What steps do you take to refer someone? When someone approaches me on LinkedIn, I scan their profile and resume to figure out if their skills and relevant experience match the job role. If they are a match, I submit their details on my company portal.

14. Do you fill out the details yourself, and is it a one-click submission? We have to fill out minimal details ourselves, such as uploading the resume, providing basic communication info, total years of experience, and any career gaps. While it is intended to be a simple submission, I think the process could be simplified further.

15. Has a hiring manager or recruiter ever reached out to you after a submission? No, the hiring team does not contact me; they directly reach out to the candidates. I can only check the candidate's status (like on hold, rejected, or in interview rounds) via the referral portal.

16. In one word, how easy or annoying is the referral process? It is somewhere in the middle. It involves a lot of back-and-forth because candidates often fail to provide necessary information like their date of birth, career gaps, and their last employer.

17. What frustrates you the most about referral requests? It is frustrating having to constantly ask candidates for additional details, as many assume that just sending a resume and phone number is enough. Having to manually extract and fill out all their skills from their resume into the portal is also highly frustrating and shouldn't be required.

18. Do you ever get too many requests from someone or a lot of people? No, I don’t.

19. Do referral bonuses matter to you? Yes, they do, but they are not my primary objective for referring someone.

20. Do you clearly know how rewards or bonuses work, and is it documented somewhere? Yes, the incentive process is documented universally in the company policy. An employee must stay with the company for at least one year for the referrer to get the retention bonus.

21. What makes you trust a candidate enough to refer them? The way a candidate first approaches me says a lot about them. Additionally, analyzing their LinkedIn profile and resume helps gauge their integrity; if they exaggerate their skills and the details don't connect, it ruins their credibility.

22. Would you refer a complete stranger? Why or why not? Yes, I would love to refer a complete stranger if they fulfill all the criteria, make a good impression, and display integrity.

23. What would make you much more likely to refer people? I would appreciate a system that provides an automated matching score or feedback on whether a candidate matches a job profile, saving me the time of manually digging through their resume.

24. What would reduce your overall effort in the referral flow? The process should be split so the referrer only fills out minimal data, while a link is sent to the candidate to fill out their own personal details, which would cut the referrer's effort by 50%. Additionally, the portal should allow creating a universal candidate profile rather than forcing us to repeat the entire manual process for every single job ID.

25. Can you refer someone for different levels, domains, or locations? Yes, I can refer candidates for any level (L1, L2, L3) and any domain (frontend or backend), as long as it falls under the Specialist Programmer category. I can also refer them for any location across India.

26. Are there any other details candidates often fail to provide? Yes, candidates are required to choose three preferred interview locations in the portal, which is another detail they rarely provide upfront, forcing me to ask them manually.
