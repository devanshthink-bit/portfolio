# Candidate 1 — interview transcript

**Who:** UI engineer, job seeker (Citibank, Adobe, Razorpay attempts)
**Source:** FigJam `CPoRM8dMgCywFz0ZbAfA6A`, section Interview Transcripts, node 488:5531. Copied verbatim; only split into one line per question.
**Real interview:** yes (confirmed by Devansh, 17 Sep 2026)

---

1. Could you please tell me about the last time you tried to get a referral? For the last time I tried to get a referral, it was not a successful one I was not referred by the candidate although they promised they would. It was for a profile in Citibank. I approached them for an open vacancy, sent my resume and details, but they could not refer me for whatever reason. The last successful attempt, where I was actually referred, was for an Adobe UI engineer profile. I asked a person from my friend list, and I got a confirmation mail that it was referred properly.

2. So basically, as far as I understood, it was for a UI developer in Adobe and in Citibank, right? Yes, exactly.

3. How did you decide who to reach out to, to ask for a referral? The first instinct is to look within your circle if you have mutual connections or anyone from your friend circle in that organization. That works out very well. If not, the second approach is searching on LinkedIn using the company filter to look for any mutual connections or employees of that company.

4. For the mentioned two companies, how did you look out for people? For the Adobe one, fortunately, I had a friend from my college days (a junior) so I reached out to her. For Citibank, I had a mutual connection who wasn't directly in my circle, but we had mutuals, so I reached out to her based on that.

5. And for Citibank and Adobe, in both cases it was a mutual connection from your close circle, right? Yes, both were mutual connections.

6. Where did you exactly contact them if you can remember? Was it on LinkedIn, WhatsApp, or any other platform? I contacted both off of LinkedIn. I had a WhatsApp contact for my friend for the Adobe one, and the same goes for the Citibank connection.

7. So you preferred WhatsApp for both? Yes, I preferred WhatsApp for both because I had their contacts. Reaching out via a personalized messaging app feels a bit more connected.

8. So it’s safe to assume, it was kind of an informal message you dropped to each of them? Yes, it was kind of an informal help I tried to ask from them.

9. How did you write your message? Did you take any help from any tool or did you craft it yourself? To be honest, I did not write any formal message. I just forwarded my resume and the contact details they needed to fill in the referral form. They didn't ask for anything else.

10. So you didn't pitch formally. You just gave all your details and resume and that’s it? Yes, exactly.

11. Did you do anything to make them aware of your context that you are asking for a referral, or did they just understand by the resume? I pinged them with the job profile link and asked, "Could you please refer me to this profile?" I mentioned that my experience and skill set matched, and asked them to go through my resume and refer me if they felt it was good.

12. So you provided them the job link? Yes, I actually provided them the job link.

13. Where did you find that link? I navigated to that link through LinkedIn's job section. If it redirects to the company portal, I copy that link and send it.

14. If I can summarize: you look for open jobs on LinkedIn, navigate to the particular company's job section, copy the link, and send it to someone you know in the company, right? Yes, and also having an understanding from our friend circles about who is in which company helps.

15. Did you feel confident or awkward asking for it, and why? I felt confident asking for it and didn't feel awkward because I already knew both of them. However, I’ll share another experience from a year ago when I asked for a referral for Razorpay. For that, I approached strangers on LinkedIn. I sent a formal message detailing my qualifications, resume, and request. I didn't hear back from them, which makes sense since my message probably wasn't even seen.

16. Okay, so you didn't hear back. No, the point of hearing back only arises when someone actually sees your message. My message was not even read.

17. How many people did you message actually from the same company? It was two or three people.

18. So nobody replied, and all of them were strangers to you? Yes, nobody replied, and all of them were strangers.

19. If I am not wrong, I can label it as you felt a bit weird asking strangers for it, but confident when asking close ones? I wouldn't call it awkward, but "weird." When you approach someone for the first time, you don't know how it will be received. There's a weirdness and uncertainty about asking a stranger for a favor.

20. What do you think makes a good referral request? A good referral request should originate from matching skill sets. The skill sets and experience should match the requirement. The process should be streamlined maybe a portal where there is a dedicated referral section. A request is good when it starts from a skill match rather than just referring someone randomly.

21. So according to you, a good skill-to-job fit matching makes a good referral request? Yes.

22. Right now, what do you think is broken in the system? The skill-to-job matching is very weird. The referral program is currently heavily focused on incentives. People just refer 10-20 profiles blindly, hoping someone gets selected so they can cash in on the referral bonus, without caring about whether the candidate's skills actually match the job.

23. Overall, what's the count of people you have reached out to and the percentage who have replied? I would say I have reached out to around 15 to 20 people in total. Out of that, I heard back from just 5. And out of those 5, 2 or 3 were from my own circle.

24. Apart from Citibank, Razorpay, and Adobe, did you ask anyone else from any other company? Yes, in the past, but the scenario was more or less similar.

25. From the people you heard back from, what usually happens after you ask? I didn't have any detailed discussions with anyone about why I was a good fit. They either ghosted or, in the case of my close contacts, they just applied directly on my behalf and I received the confirmation email.

26. So your close ones just applied it on the portal, and the strangers ghosted? Yes, exactly. I got confirmation emails when the close ones submitted it.

27. What's the hardest part about getting referrals? Getting noticed. Most of the time, when approaching strangers, the request is not even seen or heard.

28. At times when you have been ignored or ghosted, how did that make you feel? It made me feel weird in the beginning. It felt like a strong rejection. It made me feel like I shouldn't have approached strangers and should have tried harder to find mutual connections instead.

29. So you basically regretted the decision of messaging them? I didn't regret it, but I felt it would have been more fruitful if I had followed the mutual connection route.

30. Did you follow up with people who ghosted you? I might have followed up initially, but the reply rate remained the same.

31. What frustrates you most in the process? If they are ghosting or if my request isn't even visible, that's frustrating. At the very least, if someone isn't able to refer you or doesn't want to, there should be clear communication so the candidate can move on and approach someone else.

32. Have you ever sent messages to a lot of people at once? No, I have not tried bulk messaging. At max, I reach out to two or three people at a time.

33. Do you prefer reaching out to known people or strangers? I prefer known people because the failure rate with strangers is very high. Reaching out to mutuals increases the chances of completing the process.

34. What exactly makes you trust that a stranger on LinkedIn can actually help you? I filter out profiles and check how socially active they are on LinkedIn. I look at whether they comment on posts, how active their activity section is, and the endorsements or recommendations they have received. It gives you a vibe about the person's personality and whether they'd be helpful.

35. So the activity of the person and the recommendations they've received are your two pointers? Yes.

36. What are one or two things you include in your pitch or message that make you feel more confident while asking? I like to include my passion and enjoyment for the work I do, and the sense of ownership I take in my projects. I think those are rare qualities that make a strong impact.

37. Is there anything that, if it existed in the referral process, would make you feel much more confident before asking someone? A matching score. If there was an application where I could provide my resume and the job description, and it gave me a match percentage (e.g., 90%), I could share that score with the referrer. It would give both of us confidence that I am a highly compatible candidate.

38. Hypothetically, apart from skills, what else should be considered in that matching score? Apart from the skill set, it should consider the impact created in past roles, any extra-curricular domain willingness, or external projects. It shows how willing a candidate is to go beyond just their standard job duties.

39. What do you think would make this process a lot easier for you? A platform specifically connecting people looking for referrals with employees who are actually interested in referring. If there was a streamlined portal dedicated to referrals where you know the other person is open to doing it, the process would move forward without upfront rejection.

40. So you would prefer a platform where only willing referrers are present, with better clarity and conversion? Yes, and there should be visibility. I want to know if my profile was shortlisted, rejected, or if the referral was even successfully made.

41. How exactly would you like to receive updates on visibility? On emails or any other means? The user should have a choice. Personally, I think emails are a much more professional approach for job-related updates.

42. Is there anything else you would like to add? No, I think I shouldn't overcomplicate what I've already discussed. That should be all on this front.
