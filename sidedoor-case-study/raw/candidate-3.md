# Akash, as a candidate — interview transcript

**Who:** Akash, speaking as a job seeker (Product Designer and UX Designer roles: a Gurgaon tech company, a Noida design studio)
**Source:** FigJam `CPoRM8dMgCywFz0ZbAfA6A`, section Interview Transcripts, node 488:5533. Copied verbatim; only split into one line per question.
**Real interview:** yes

1. Can you share the last time you tried getting a referral? Well, the last time was a bit frustrating. I reached out for a Product Designer role at a company in Gurgaon. I sent my portfolio and resume, but the person never actually submitted it, even though they said they would. Also, right before that, I had a successful referral for a UX Designer position at a design studio in Noida. I asked a former colleague, and I got the confirmation the next day.

2. How did you choose who to contact for a referral? My first instinct is always to look for other UX or product people. I feel like fellow designers understand the struggle of putting together a portfolio. If I don't have direct friends there, I use LinkedIn to search for mutual conections, specifically looking for Senior Designers or Design Managers in that specific company.

3. How did you find people in these two cases? For the Noida studio, it was straightforward because I had an ex-colleague who had joined them a few months ago, so I just pinged him. For the Gurgaon company, I didn't know anyone directly, so I had to search on LinkedIn to find a mutual connection who was working in their product team.

4. So in both situations, the person was somehow connected to your circle, right? Mostly, yeah. The Noida one was a direct ex-colleague, so very close circle. The Gurgaon one was a second-degree connection we shared a couple of mutual friends from my college, so there was a little bit of common thing there.

5. Where did you reach out to them? I contacted the Gurgaon connection on LinkedIn. But for my ex-colleague at the Noida studio, I just texted him directly on WhatsApp since we still talk occasionally.

6. So for close connections, you chose WhatsApp? Yeah, absolutely. I feel like if you have their number, WhatsApp cuts through the noise. It feels much more personal. Plus, people check WhatsApp a hundred times a day, whereas LinkedIn messages might get ignored.

7. So you sent them a casual message? Yes. With my former coworker, it was super casual. I just asked how he was doing, complimented some of the recent case studies the studio published, and then casually asked if he could drop my portfolio link in their internal system.

8. How did you frame your message? Did you use any tool for help? I just typed it out myself. I didn't use ChatGPT or anything because I didn't want it to sound like AI. I just sent him a quick text, followed by my Figma portfolio link, my resume, and the specific job ID they needed for their portal.

9. So instead of pitching yourself, you mainly shared your details and resume? Exactly. Because he already knew my work ethic and how I approach UX problems, I didn't need to do the whole "here is why I'm a great fit" speech. I just gave him the details he needed to fill out the form.

10. Did you explain why you were asking for a referral, or did they understand from the resume itself? I just gave him the direct link to the career page and said, "Hey, I saw you guys are hiring for this specific UX role, I’d love to throw my hat in the ring. Could you refer me?" So the context was very clear right from the first message.

11. So you also shared the job link with them? Yes, I made sure to send the exact URL to the job posting so he wouldn't have to go hunting for it on their internal portal.

12. Where did you get that job link from? I initially saw the opening on a design community board, but I went to the studio's official website, found their careers page, and copied the direct link from there to make sure it was the recent listing.

13. So basically, you search for jobs, open the company’s careers page, copy the link, and send it to someone working there, right? Yes. It’s all about reducing the friction for the person helping you out. If I want a referral, I need to do 90% of the work for them so all they have to do is copy and paste my details.

14. Did you feel comfortable or awkward while asking for the referral, and why? With my ex-colleague, I was totally confident. But asking the mutual connection for the Gurgaon tech company felt pretty awkward. You're basically dropping into a stranger's DMs and saying, "Hey, we know the same person, can you vouch for my career?" It always feels a bit transactional and uncomfortable.

15. Okay, so it felt somewhat transactional to you? Yeah, exactly. Especially when they don't reply right away. You sit there overthinking your message, wondering if you came off as too pushy or if your portfolio wasn't strong enough. It definitely messes with your head a bit.

16. Around how many people from the same company did you contact? For that Gurgaon tech role, I probably reached out to three or four different people in their product and design departments, hoping at least one of them would check their LinkedIn messages.

17. So none of them really replied properly, and they were mostly strangers? Yeah, pretty much. Out of the four, three completely ghosted me. One guy replied saying he would submit it, but then he never followed up, and I never got an email confirmation. They were all second-degree strangers.

18. So would it be correct to say you felt comfortable asking known people but awkward asking strangers? That is the perfect way to label it. Asking a friend feels like a collaborative effort. Asking a stranger feels like you are begging for a favor, and the weirdness of it makes the whole process pretty stressful.

19. In your opinion, what makes a referral request good? A good request shows that you've actually done your homework. You shouldn't just ask blindly. You need to connect the dots between the problem statements they are working on in that role and the specific case studies in your portfolio. It needs to be a clear, obvious match.

20. So according to you, strong skill-to-job alignment makes a referral request better? Yes, absolutely. If I am applying for a high-level UX role, but my portfolio only shows basic graphic design, it makes the referrer look bad for recommending me. The fit has to be undeniable.

21. At the moment, what do you think is wrong with the referral system? The system is just broken because it's become a volume game rather than a quality game. People just blindly refer anyone to get the cash bonus if the person gets hired. So HR teams are still flooded with bad resumes, even from the referrals.

22. Roughly how many people have you contacted overall, and what percentage replied back? Over the last few months of job hunting, I’ve probably messaged around 25 to 30 people. I'd say maybe 6 or 7 replied. So my response rate sits somewhere around 20 to 25 percent, which honestly isn't great.

23. Besides the Gurgaon company and the Noida studio, did you contact people from other companies too? Oh yeah, a bunch. I reached out to folks at Swiggy, a few fintech startups, and some international agencies that hire remote designers. The ghosting ratio is pretty consistent across the board.

24. Usually, what happens after someone responds to your referral request? Usually, there's no real conversation about my design skills. They either drop off entirely, or they say "Done" and I get a generic automated email from their HR system saying my profile was submitted. There’s rarely any meaningful conversation.

25. So your known contacts submitted the referral, while strangers mostly ignored you? Essentially, yes. The ex-colleague submitted it and I got the automated email the next day. The strangers on LinkedIn either ignored my note completely or made a promise to do it and then disappeared.

26. What do you find most difficult about getting referrals? The hardest part is simply getting a stranger to care. You are just a message on their screen. Convincing them to stop their day, open your links, and navigate their internal portal for a person they don't know is a task for them.

27. When people ignored or ghosted you, how did it affect you? It feels pretty demoralizing, to be honest. Imposter syndrome kicks in hard. You start thinking if your portfolio is terrible or if you aren't good enough. It feels like a rejection of your entire professional worth, even if they were just too busy to reply.

28. So did you end up feeling that messaging them was not worth it? I wouldn't say I regretted it, because you have to try anyway. But it definitely made me feel exhausted. It makes you realize that cold outreach has a very low ROI on your emotional energy.

29. Did you ever send follow-up messages to people who ignored you? Sometimes I try to send a gentle follow-up after a week, like "Hey, just floating this to the top of your inbox." But it rarely changes the result. If they ignored the first one, they usually ignore the follow-up too.

30. What frustrates you the most during this process? The lack of transparency. I hate sending my portfolio into a black hole. If someone isn't going to refer me, or if my skills aren't a match, I just wish they would say "No, sorry." The whole duration of not knowing is way more frustrating than a direct rejection.

31. Have you ever messaged many people at the same time for referrals? No, I never copy-paste too many messages. That feels spammy. I usually just target two or three specific people per company, and I customize the message for each one based on their profile.

32. Do you prefer asking people you know or complete strangers? Known people, 100%. The conversion rate is so much better, and there is no awkwardness. With strangers, you spend so much time crafting the perfect message just to be ignored, which is too draining.

33. What makes you believe a stranger on LinkedIn might actually help you? I look at their recent activity. If they frequently post about design, comment on other people's posts, or have a lot of recommendations from their peers, it tells me they are actually engaged with the community and might be willing to help out a fellow designer.

34. So the person’s activity and recommendations are the main things you check? Yes, exactly. It helps me filter out the inactive profiles. If someone hasn't posted or commented in six months, there's no point in sending them a referral request because they probably won't even see it.

35. What are one or two things you include in your message that make you feel more confident while asking? I always try to highlight a specific problem statement I noticed their company is working on, and then link directly to a project in my Figma portfolio where I solved a similar issue. It shows I'm not just looking for any job, I'm looking for their job.

36. Is there something that could exist in the referral process that would make you feel more confident before reaching out? A matching score or an alignment tool would be great. If I could paste my resume and their job description into a tool, and it told me "You are a 92% match based on your UX skills," I would feel much more confident sharing it with a stranger to prove I'm worth their time.

37. Other than skills, what else do you think should be part of that matching score? It should also include design philosophy and cultural fit. Like, have I worked in fast-paced startups or slow enterprise environments? It should also consider extra-curriculars, like if I write design blogs or contribute to open-source UI kits. That shows genuine passion for the craft.

38. What do you think could make the referral process much easier for you? Maybe a marketplace or platform where employees raise their hand and say, "I have the bandwidth to refer 3 designers this week." That way, the job seeker isn't guessing who is willing to help. You'd completely eliminate the fear of rejection and ghosting.

39. So you would rather use a platform with willing referrers, better transparency, and higher chances of conversion? Exactly. It would save everyone so much time. I wouldn't feel like asking for favors randomly, and the referrers wouldn't get spammed. Plus, I’d want real-time visibility. I want to see a progress bar so I know if HR actually opened my portfolio.

40. How would you prefer getting updates or visibility about your referral status? On emails or any other means? I think email is the most reliable, but a push notification from this hypothetical platform would be cool too. As long as it's an immediate update that my status has changed, I’m happy.

41. Is there anything more you would like to share? Not much. The whole process just needs to be more humanized and transparent. It was really great talking through this, hope it helps your research!
