# Atishya, as a referrer — interview transcript

**Who:** Atishya, senior product manager at a consumer tech company in Gurgaon, speaking as someone who refers
**Source:** FigJam `CPoRM8dMgCywFz0ZbAfA6A`, section Interview Transcripts, node 488:5529. Copied verbatim; only split into one line per question.
**Real interview:** yes

1. Hey, thanks for doing this. To start, can you just give me a quick background on what you do? Yeah, no problem. I’m a senior product manager right now. I work at this consumer tech company in Gurgaon.. can I mention the name as well? no worries its fine. Ok and I’ve been here for about three years, and my day-to-day is mostly focused on user growth and figuring out how to keep people coming back to our app.

2. So, as a senior guy there, I imagine you get asked for referrals. How often is that happening? Yes yes, it’s everyday thing now. On a normal day, I probably get around 10-20 messages from people asking me to refer them.

3. Wow, 20 a day? Does it ever get worse than that? Yeah, it gets crazy if our team actually posts a public job opening. Like if we put out a listing for a junior PM or a new designer, I get alot of DMs. I can easily get 40 or 50 DMs in just a few days.

4. How do you even manage that many messages while trying to do your actual job? Honestly, sometimes I can't. I literally have to turn off my LinkedIn notifications. otherwise I can't even see the important messages from the people I actually work with.

5. Who are all these people messaging you? Are they mostly people you know? Not at all. I’d say maybe 5 percent of them are guys I went to college with, or people I used to work with at my old company. The other 95 percent are complete strangers.

6. So they are just random people finding you on LinkedIn? Exactly. People I have never spoken to in my life. They just type our company name into the search bar, filter by the "Product" team, and find my profile. And I know they are just copying and pasting the exact same message to me and most other PMs on my team.

7. Because you get so much spam, do you ever just completely ignore people? Well honestly, I ghost people all the time. I used to feel kind of bad about it when I first started, but now, I just have to do it anyhow. There's just not enough time in the day to reply to everyone.

8. What is the main reason you will just instantly ghost a stranger? It's just how their message is. If I open it and the first line says "Dear Sir" and then it's this massive essay explaining their whole life story from high school to now... I just skip it.It takes too much time to read all that.

9. What if they don't include the job they want? Does that happen? All the time! I just ignore or delete them. People will message me saying "Please refer me to your company," but they don't give me a job link. If you make me do the detective work to figure out where you fit, Sorry but I'm not going to reply.

10. What if someone wants a job that is completely outside of your area, like in Finance? I usually just ignore those too. If a random stranger asks me for a finance analyst job, I don't know how to read their resume. I don't know what makes a good finance person. I only refer strangers for Product, Design, or Tech, because I can just look at their past work and know if they are up to role requirement.

11. Okay, so what makes you actually want to reply to a stranger? What does a good message look like? A short, to-the-point message is the best. If they say something like, "Hey, I saw your team just launched this new feature. I worked on a similar problem at my last startup. I see an opening for a PM role, here is the exact job ID. Here are three quick bullet points on what I achieved, and my resume attached." That would work for me atleast.

12. Why does that specific format work so well for you? Because it shows they respect my time. It shows me they are genuine, they actually researched what my team does, and they didn't make me hunt for the job link. If someone sends me that, I will refer them right away.

13. Do you treat your real-life friends differently when they ask for a favor? Oh yeah, for sure honestly.

14. What about the referral bonus money? Does that make you want to just refer as many people as possible? I mean, it's a nice perk. If you refer a senior PM or a developer and they get hired, the bonus is good. But it’s a kind of a gamble again.

15. Why is it a gamble? Because the person has to pass like 4-5 rounds of interviews, actually accept the job offer, not back out at the last minute, and then they have to stay at the company for six whole months before I get the bonus. Since the chances of a random stranger from LinkedIn actually making it that far are really low, the money isn't a strong reason to refer.

16. Let's talk about the actual process. Say you decide to refer someone. What do you physically have to do? Well it’s the worst part. We use this HR software, I won't name the brand, but it sucks honestly.

17. Walk me through it step-by-step. What happens first? First, I have to log into our company portal. Then I have to approve a notification on my phone. Then I have to find the internal jobs tab, which take a lot of time to load. Then I have to manually search for the specific job number they gave me.

18. That already sounds annoying. What happens when you upload their resume? I upload the PDF, and half the time, the system completely cant handle their information. It puts their phone number in the name box and stuff like that. So then I'm sitting there, manually typing out their first name, last name, phone number, and email by hand. And then at the end, there's this mandatory quiz asking me exactly how I know the person. It takes like 10 or 15 minutes per person.

19. Does having to do all that manual typing stop you from referring more people? 100%. If it was just a one-click thing, I’d probably refer way more people. But because I know it’s going to cost me 15 minutes of entering their data, I tend to avoid it mostly.

20. Do the candidates ever forget to give you some of the info you need for those forms? Oh yes, all the time. They always send the resume, but they forget the basic info. The portal makes me type where they currently live, if they are willing to relocate to Gurgaon, and exactly how many days their notice period is.

21. So what do you do when you don't have that info? I have to go back to LinkedIn, message them, and wait for them to answer. And half the time, while I'm waiting and kept the tab open, I remember the HR portal logs me out for being inactive. So then I have to do the whole login thing all over again. It is really frustrating.

22. Once you finally hit the submit button, what happens next? Do you get updates? Once I click submit, I just get an automated email and then... nothing. I will never hear from the recruiting team again.

23. You never find out if they got an interview or got rejected? No, Not from HR. If I want to know the result, I have to log back into the portal and check their status myself. And usually, the status just says "Under Review" for like three months and one random day it changes to "Rejected." Nobody from HR ever gives me any context on why the person didn't get it.

24. Do the candidates message you asking for updates during that time? Yeah, they do multiple times but I have to tell them I literally have no idea why it’s stuck.

25. Do you ever try asking the recruiters for an update? Yeah sometimes I do ping the recruiter on our internal Slack, but they are dealing with hundreds of candidates, so they almost never reply to me.

26. Looking at the big picture, do you think this referral system even works to find good talent anymore? Honestly, right now? No. It's not about finding the best talent anymore, it's just about who is the most fast at networking and spamming in linkedin messages.

27. Why do you think candidates are acting so aggressively right now? Because the regular job application portals are totally broken. Desperate candidates feel like they have to beg strangers for referrals just to get someone to look at their resume. Maybe that’s why.

28. If you had the power to completely redesign the referral process for your company from scratch, what is the very first thing you would change? Oh, I’ve thought about this actually. All I want as someone referring is zero typing for me. None. I should just have my own unique referral link, that I can just copy and paste to them in a chat.

29. So the candidate would do the work? Yes exactly. They click my link, they fill out all the forms, they upload their own resume, they answer all the questions... and then the system just pings me. I just look at it, click "Approve," and that's it. It should take me few seconds instead of 15 minutes.

30. What else would you add to this dream system? I would want an automated feedback loop. There should be a dashboard where I can see exactly where they are, did HR call them? Are they taking interviews? Did they get the offer? all these things.

31. And what about when they get rejected? If they get rejected, the recruiter should be forced to pick a reason for it from a drop-down menu like "Salary mismatch" or something else and the system should automatically email that reason to the candidate & the employee who referred. This way, the candidate will actually know why they didn't get it, and it would make whole process so much easier.

32. That's all for now. Thanks a lot! You have been really kind to answer all my questions. It's been a pleasure Devansh.
