# Akash, as a referrer — interview transcript

**Who:** Akash, senior backend developer at a finance startup, speaking as someone who refers
**Source:** FigJam `CPoRM8dMgCywFz0ZbAfA6A`, section Interview Transcripts, node 488:5528. Copied verbatim; only split into one line per question.
**Real interview:** yes

1. Tell me a bit about your current role and if you're actively involved in referring people at your company? Yeah, so, I’m a senior backend dev right now at this finance startup. Mostly working on the payment side of things. And yeah, I definitely do referrals. Honestly, like, whenever we’re hiring, HR is always pushing us to bring people in, so I try to help out when I’m not too occupied with work.

2. If you had to guess, how many referral requests do you get in a typical month?  It really fluctuates based on what's happening. When the market is down, maybe I get one or two a month. When its hiring season, I get a lot of requests in DMs. I'd say, around 15 to 20 requests a month across different platforms, which is bit too much to manage.

3. Do you actually entertain requests from random people on the internet, or do you stick to your own circle?  I do a mix of both, but I prefer my own network. With ex-colleagues or college juniors, the trust is already there. I will refer strangers, but my filter for them is kinda high. If a stranger's resume isn't ~90% match for the job description, I rather not proceed with it.

4. What's the typical channel people use to slide into your DMs for a referral?  For the people I know in real life, it's almost always WhatsApp or maybe a quick call. They just ping me like, 'Hey, saw a role, can you push my CV?' For strangers, it's 100% LinkedIn DMs. I rarely get cold emails.

5. Walk me through exactly what happens in your head when you get a cold message asking for a referral. How do you evaluate them?  The first thing I do is look at their current job title and their tech stack. If we are hiring a Java dev and their resume have only Python projects, I instantly close it. Only if the tech stack aligns perfectly and they wrote a polite message, I'll consider doing it.

6. Have you ever felt like your own professional reputation is on the line when you put someone's name in the system?  You know, officially HR says there's no penalty, but behind the scenes, I think yes. If I refer a guy and he completely underperforms at the technical interview or turns out to be rude to the panel, it reflects on me. I don't want them to think I just spam the portal with poor candidates just to get the bonus.

7. Let's talk about the actual internal tool you use. What is the process of submitting a referral like for you?  We use a third-party ATS. I have to log in, go to the internal job board, find the job ID which is a pain to search and then manually upload their resume. Sometimes the resume parser fails, and I have to manually type in their name, email, and phone number myself.

8. What is the single most annoying part of using that internal referral portal?  The manual data entry part. Also, it forces me to write a 'Recommendation Reason' in a mandatory text box. If it's a stranger, I literally have to make up something generic based on their resume, like 'Strong background in microservices architecture.' It feels unnecessary.

9. Do you ever find yourself doing the administrative work for the candidate, like chasing them for missing details?  Yes It happens so often and its really frustrating. They send the resume, but the portal requires their current location, their official notice period, and sometimes a link to their GitHub. So then I have to DM them back, wait for a reply. It breaks my own focus.

10. What specific details do candidates usually forget to send you in their first message? How much notice period they have, and the actual job ID. If they don't give me the link or the ID number, I have follow through or go digging around searching for it. And I hate doing that.

11. Do you get notified by HR or the hiring manager about what’s happening with the people you referred? No I only get this email saying "Thanks for submitting," and then... nothing. The only way I find out if they actually got an interview is if I go back into the portal myself and look them up, or if the guy actually messages me to say he got a call.

12. Do you ever feel guilty if a referral doesn't go through, and how do you communicate that to the candidate? Yeah, a little bit. If it's a close one, I'll maybe text them. But for some stranger, I don't even tell them cuz I don’t have time to do it, honestly. If they message me asking for an update, then maybe I'll go check and tell them they didn't get it.

13. Honestly, how much does the financial referral bonus actually motivate you to do this? Look, if someone tells you the money doesn't matter, they're lying. The bonus for a senior tech role here is... it's a lot of money, honestly. That's definitely one of the main reason I would want to do the submission on portal. But, I mean, I will not refer a really poor candidate just for bonus. Borderline can be done still.

14. Have you ever had a situation where you completely ignored or ghosted a request? Why did you do it? Honestly, all the time. Like, if someone just sends a message that's like, "Hi sir, please see attached resume" and there's no job link, no "how are you," nothing... I just delete it. They don’t understand I'm a developer, not a recruiter. I'm not figure out myself what job you're good for. If you make me do all the work, I'm just gonna ignore you.

15. Is there a difference in how you treat a request from an old college buddy versus a LinkedIn stranger? Oh, yes. Big difference. Like, if my buddy sends me a bad resume, I'll literally call him and ask him to fix this before I send it. But if a stranger sends me a bad resume? I just close it. Knowing each other surely helps, I think its true for everyone.

16. What are some massive red flags in a resume or a message that make you instantly say 'no'? Spelling mistakes in the very first message is a big one. And, oh man, if they send a Word doc instead of a PDF, that just looks so bad to me. Also, sometimes I see their LinkedIn profile says one thing, but the resume they sent me has totally different dates or jobs. That just feels problematic and I'll pass it.

17. Can you refer across different departments? Like if you're in tech, can you refer a marketing person? I mean, technically yeah. On the system I can see all the jobs everywhere in the company. But generally won’t do it for people I don't know. Like, I don't know about what makes a good marketing resume or a sales guy, so it feels weird. I'd probably only do it for a non-tech job if it's a really good friend I actually trust.

18. If I were to build a magic tool to fix this whole mess for you (the referrer), what is the number one feature it needs to have? It can have a form or link which I can send them and then they have to type in all their info and attach the files. And then it just sends me a quick notification where I click "yes" or "no." which just... stop making me do all the typing.

19. What's a dream scenario for you when a candidate reaches out? What does the perfect message look like? Best case would be something like, "Hey, we know the same people. I have this specific job ID with the link. I have the main skills you need, here's my PDF resume or portfolio. Let me know if you need more." Just... short, to the point, no extra long stories, and I don't have to reply and ask for more stuff.

20. Any final thoughts on the whole referral culture in the tech industry right now? I don't know, it just feels kind of broken right now. It's just a numbers game for people looking for jobs, like they're just spamming everyone. And for us on the inside, the software we use feels like it's from 15 years ago. If someone could just make a tool that doesn't waste everyone's time, it'd be so much better for everybody.
