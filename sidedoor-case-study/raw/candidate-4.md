# Atishya, as a candidate — interview transcript

**Who:** Atishya, speaking as a job seeker (Product Manager at Atlassian, Product Designer at CRED)
**Source:** FigJam `CPoRM8dMgCywFz0ZbAfA6A`, section Interview Transcripts, node 488:5534. Copied verbatim; only split into one line per question.
**Real interview:** yes

1. How are you feeling about the whole job hunt and networking process right now? Honestly, it is just mentally exhausting. It’s really tiring having to put on this fake, happy networking face every single day. You just want to do your actual work, but you are kind of forced to play this social game just to even get an interview.

2. How many people have you actually tried reaching out to lately? Over the last two months, I think I’ve messaged around 40 people. I've tried people at Microsoft, Flipkart, and a bunch of different startups too.

3. And out of those 40 people, how many actually get back to you? Very few. I’d say maybe 8 or 9 people actually replied to me. And out of those, only about 3-4 actually went ahead and put my resume in their system.

4. Do you ever feel bad or regret sending those messages when they don't reply? I don't really regret it because you literally don't have a choice right now. If you don't message people, you have a zero percent chance of getting the job. If you do message them, maybe you have a 1 percent chance. I just hate that this is the system we have to use.

5. When people ignore you, how does it affect you? At first, it really hurts. You take it personally and start thinking maybe your portfolio is just bad. But honestly, after the 20th time someone ignores you, you just become numb to it. You realize it’s not really about you, people are just overwhelmed with their own work.

6. Do you try to message a ton of people at once using any software? No, no. I don't use any of those automated tools that sends out a hundred messages in bulk. It's way too risky. I do it manually, maybe just 3 or 4 people a day so I can actually personalize it a bit.

7. Who do you prefer to reach out to? People you know or complete strangers? Oh, people I know, for sure. The success rate is just so much better. You don't have to pretend to be someone you're not. Plus, someone you know will actually warn you if their company is a bad place to work before you join.

8. What is your normal process for finding someone to ask? I usually start by scanning my own network. I'll go through my phone contacts or look at my mutuals on Instagram to see if anyone is working at the company I want. If that completely fails, then I go to LinkedIn, type in the company name, and look for people to see if I can find any kind of connection.

9. When you have to message a total stranger on LinkedIn, how do you pick who to message? I try to look at their overall vibe on their profile. Like, do they post about helping juniors? Do they share good resources? If their profile is just them bragging about their own promotions all the time, I figure they probably won't help me, so I skip them.

10. So you just look at what they post on their feed? Yeah, their recent activity is the biggest clue. If they are leaving nice, encouraging comments on other people's posts, they probably have some empathy. They just feel like a safer bet to message.

11. When you write a message to a stranger, do you try to prove your skills? Yeah, I try to include at least one hard metric. Instead of just saying "I'm a good designer," I will say something like "I redesigned a flow that increased conversions by 15%." I feel like giving them a real number makes me look like less of a risk for them to refer.

12. Why do you think that skill matching is so important to them? Because if it's obvious that I'm a good fit for the job, the person doesn't feel like they are risking their own reputation at the company by recommending me. It has to make sense for them.

13. Let's talk about some recent examples. Can you tell me about the last time you tried asking a stranger? Yeah, about three weeks ago I tried for a Product Manager role at Atlassian. I messaged this guy on LinkedIn, but it went absolutely nowhere. He just left me on read.

14. Did you have any connection to him at all? Technically we went to the same college, so on paper we had a connection, but in real life we were complete strangers. We had never met.

15. How did you write that specific message to him? Well, LinkedIn has character limit for a connection request, which is tough. I actually used ChatGPT a little bit just to help me fit my experience into that, without sounding too desperate. Then I changed some words to make it sound more like me.

16. How did you try to get his attention? I tried to build a little connection first. I said, "Hey, I saw we both graduated from this college, really love what you guys did with Jira," and then I brought up the PM role.

17. Did you message anyone else at Atlassian since he didn't reply? Yeah, I think I sent requests to maybe four or five PMs in total there just for a safety net because you know most of them will ignore you.

18. What happened with those other four people? Two of them didn't even accept the connection request. Two accepted it but just ignored my actual message. And one guy replied and said "Sure, send it," but then after I sent my resume, he disappeared and never replied again.

19. That is frustrating. Do you ever message them again to follow up? I used to do that. I'd send a "Hey, just checking in" message, but honestly it almost never works. Now, if they don't reply in a week, I just cross them off my mental list and move on to the next person.

20. How does it feel asking these strangers for favors? It is so awkward. You feel like a telemarketer interrupting their day. You kind of just cringe when you hit the send button because you feel like a nuisance.

21. What usually happens after a stranger actually submits your referral? They just say "Done, good luck" and then you are stuck waiting for some automated email from their HR system. Sometimes it takes days, sometimes it never even arrives. They have no real accountability to you.

22. Okay, what about a time a referral actually worked out recently? About a couple of months ago, I got one for a PD role at CRED. That one went really well.

23. How did you find someone for that one? It was actually easy. A guy who was my senior in college posted about his new job there on his Instagram story. So I just replied to it.

24. Where did you guys end up talking? We started on Instagram DM, but then we moved over to WhatsApp because it's just much easier to send PDF files on there.

25. Why do you prefer WhatsApp over LinkedIn for this kind of stuff? WhatsApp is just where you have real, normal conversations. LinkedIn feels so corporate. On WhatsApp, I can just use an emoji and be like "Hey man, I need a favor," and it doesn't feel like I'm writing a formal email. It's way faster.

26. So you didn't really have to pitch yourself to him? No, there was zero pitching. I think I literally just texted, "Bro, saw your company is hiring, could you help me with a referral?" He already knew my work from our college days, so I just gave him my resume and the job link.

27. Do you always provide the exact link to the job? Always. You can't ask someone to do you a favor and then expect them to go dig inside their company portal to find the right job. You have to decrease their work load.

28. Where do you grab those job links from? Usually I see the job on LinkedIn first. But LinkedIn's 'Easy Apply' is like a black hole. So I always click through to the company's actual career page, make sure the job is still open on their end, and then copy that specific link to send to the person.

29. What would you say is the most annoying thing about this whole referral process? The silence. It's like a black hole. You do all this extra work to find someone, you finally get the referral, and then the company's HR team just never contacts you. You don't get a rejection, you don't get feedback. Just nothing for months.

30. Why do you think the system is failing so much right now? I think companies just treat referrals like a regular hiring channel now. People aren't actually vouching for each other anymore. Employees just want to get the referral bonuses, so they refer literally anyone. Then HRs might still have to filter through thousands of bad referrals, which defeats the whole point.

31. If you could redesign the process, what would make it easier for you? I would love a place where employees can actually list themselves and say "I am open to referring for this job role." Then I would know for sure that I am not bothering them, because they themselves volunteered.

32. So you want a platform with willing people only? Exactly. It would completely take away all that anxiety of cold-messaging people. It would just be clean: they want to refer someone good, and I am a good candidate looking for a referral.

33. You talked about skill matching before. Would you want that in this ideal platform? Yes. If there was a verified Match Score that checked my skills, my notice period, and my salary against the job. If I had a badge that proved I was an 85% match, I wouldn't feel like I was begging. I'd feel like I was bringing them a genuinely good candidate.

34. How would you want to track your progress on a platform like that? I just want a simple dashboard like interface. Like one of those status trackers, but for my application. Let me just log in and see if it's been reviewed or if I'm getting an interview. I don't want to have to check my email 50 times a day anymore.

35. Do you have any final thoughts on all this? I have pretty much covered everything just that the HR space really needs to figure this out. The whole system is broken, and it's the job seekers who are paying the mental price for it right now.
